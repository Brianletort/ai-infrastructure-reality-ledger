## Post 1: The launch thesis

### Hook

An announced data center is not an operating data center. Treating both as capacity is how a map
becomes a confidence machine rather than an evidence tool.

### Body

I built the AI Infrastructure Reality Ledger around that distinction.

The model separates announcement, construction, activation, contest, supersession, and correction.
Each material claim is designed to carry exact evidence references, multiple timestamps, source
rights, and explicit uncertainty.

The public beta is deliberately synthetic: six fixture-derived inventory records and 100 reviewed
timelines across four test metros. It is not real market coverage. Publishing the method before
expanding the data is intentional...it lets people challenge the evidence model, interface, and
controls without confusing a product demonstration for a market conclusion.

My judgment is that the useful artifact is not another globe. It is the chain from visible claim to
source, review, and correction.

## Post 2: Evidence is a model, not a footnote

### Hook

A citation at the bottom of a page does not make the page evidence-led.

### Body

For infrastructure claims, I want five things to remain separate.

Source: who published the material, when, and under what redistribution rights.

Claim: the bounded assertion actually being made.

Evidence packet: the exact references and signals behind it.

Review: authority, directness, entity match, conflicts, and missing information.

Revision: what was corrected or superseded, without erasing the prior state.

That is the core of the Reality Ledger. It also keeps valid time, assertion time, source-publication
time, and retrieval time distinct. Those timestamps often answer different questions, and
agglomerating them creates false precision.

The beta corpus is synthetic, so it cannot validate factual accuracy. It can expose whether the
model makes unsupported transitions and missing evidence visible.

preserved” beneath the final transition.

## Post 3: The globe is an index

### Hook

The fastest way to overstate infrastructure intelligence is to put an imprecise point on a
beautiful globe and let the visual do the arguing.

### Body

The Reality Ledger uses the opposite design rule: the globe is an index into evidence.

The current scene uses local geometry, generalized coordinates, deterministic layers, and no
remote basemap. A location can lead to a metro, facility, event, or evidence packet, but it does not
imply site precision. The non-WebGL map summary remains available, and the synthetic warning stays
visible.

There are three visual themes and a timeline control because state changes over time matter. Yet
the UI does not convert headless animation cadence into a representative GPU claim. That
measurement remains inconclusive until it can be taken on actual target hardware.

Good visual engineering is partly restraint: encode what is known, disclose what is generalized,
and provide a useful path when WebGL is unavailable.

## Post 4: Public does not mean redistributable

### Hook

A public URL answers where content can be viewed. It does not answer whether that content can be
copied into an open dataset.

### Body

Source licensing is part of the Reality Ledger’s domain model.

Every source receives one disposition: `republish`, `derived-only`, `link-only`, or `prohibited`.
Attribution, publisher, terms, retrieval time, and intended use travel with the source record.
Unclear rights fail closed.

That creates friction, but it is the right friction. A derived fact can have a different
redistribution posture from the page used to support it. An official landing page may justify a
link-only manual review without justifying an automated scraper. A hash can prove byte equality;
it cannot upgrade rights.

The repository includes deterministic third-party notices and source-manifest gates. They are
operating controls, not legal advice.

### CTA

## Post 5: Activation needs a rule

### Hook

“Open,” “powered,” and “operational” are often used as if they were interchangeable. They are not.

### Body

The synthetic beta demonstrates an activation rule: at least two independent signals, one
authoritative signal, and one non-imagery signal.

That does not make the conclusion automatically true. It makes the review threshold explicit and
testable. The evidence packet still records directness, authority, entity match, timestamps, and
conflicts. A correction can later supersede the activation event without deleting the original
decision.

Why insist on a rule? Because otherwise an announcement can quietly become construction, and
construction can quietly become operating capacity through copy changes rather than new evidence.

The exact policy will need challenge when real public-source records are introduced. The beta gives
reviewers something concrete to disagree with.

## Post 6: Constraints are part of the record

### Hook

Infrastructure intelligence is often most useful when it preserves what cannot be concluded.

### Body

The current inventory has six synthetic records. Capacity is unknown for all six. Operator is
unknown for three. Coordinates are generalized. One overlapping representation remains an
unresolved conflict.

Those are not defects to be hidden in a footnote. They are first-class parts of the record.

The same applies to system constraints. The beta has no production ingestion, public hosting,
operational database, service level, or representative real-GPU measurement. Optional
PostgreSQL/PostGIS, object storage, PMTiles, and worker paths are documented without pretending they
have been provisioned.

The broader principle is simple: unknown, unavailable, prohibited, and inconclusive are different
states. A useful ledger should preserve each one.

## Post 7: Metro evidence should drill down, not roll up

### Hook

A metro total is usually the end of a chart. In the Reality Ledger, it should be the beginning of a
review.

### Body

The beta uses four synthetic deep-review metros: Northern Virginia, Dallas–Fort Worth, Phoenix, and
Toronto. Each has exactly 25 reviewed synthetic timelines.

The interface moves from metro to facility to event to evidence packet. That sequencing matters.
Aggregation can be useful, but it should not sever the path back to the records, references,
conflicts, and unknowns that produced it.

The current numbers are fixture counts, not capacity or market totals. They exist to test
navigation, review states, chronology, and evidence retrieval. Real metro coverage would require a
separate public-source replacement workflow, explicit source rights, independent review, and a
fresh coverage statement.

## Post 8: Corrections are contributions

### Hook

If an evidence system cannot make correction easy without making mutation casual, it will drift.

### Body

The Reality Ledger treats a correction as a review packet.

A contributor identifies the exact record and disputed field, proposes a change, explains the
rationale, and provides supporting evidence. Maintainers then review source rights, entity match,
evidence strength, conflicts, and downstream impact.

Accepted corrections do not erase the prior claim. They create a correction or supersession link
so the history remains inspectable.

The beta correction builder does not submit anything externally. It creates a local packet for
review. That boundary is intentional while authentication, authorization, abuse handling, and
operational ownership remain unimplemented.

visible.

### CTA

## Post 9: Performance and accessibility need honest gates

### Hook

A headless browser can catch regressions. Representative-device evidence still has to come from a
real browser and GPU.

### Body

The release harness separates measured evidence from representative evidence.

It checks Chromium, Firefox, and WebKit; responsive overflow; keyboard paths; reduced motion;
serious and critical axe findings; visual baselines; JavaScript payload; local readiness; layer
generation; playback reducer cost; and headless cadence.

The representative laptop gate now has real-browser evidence: Chrome 151 on an Apple M4 Pro Metal
renderer sustained 120.04 FPS over 10 seconds with a visible deck overlay. The representative
midrange mobile target remains unmeasured, so the overall decision remains inconclusive. The
laptop result does not convert the mobile target into a green check.

Accessibility follows the same discipline. The text fallback is not decorative. It preserves a
usable evidence path when JavaScript or WebGL is unavailable.

## Post 10: What the build changed

### Hook

The main build lesson was not technical. It was sequencing.

### Body

Starting with a broad map would have optimized for visible coverage before the evidence model was
ready to support it.

The cleaner sequence was:

Define lifecycle and time semantics. Build source and rights boundaries. Generate deterministic
synthetic fixtures. Add the evidence and correction paths. Make uncertainty visible in the
interface. Then build the globe and release gates.

That sequence exposed several places where a polished interface could outrun the underlying truth:
raw source fields in a public contract, unclear package dispositions, generalized coordinates that
look exact, and performance targets that headless measurement cannot substantiate.

The public beta still has meaningful gaps. That is acceptable because they are explicit. The next
phase should replace synthetic scope only where sources, rights, review, and correction controls
are strong enough to carry it.

### CTA
