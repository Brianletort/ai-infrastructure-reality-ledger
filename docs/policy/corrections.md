# Correction policy

## Principles

The ledger is corrigible, traceable, and non-destructive. A correction does not erase the historical
record. It records the challenged object, evidence, disposition, decision time, and any replacement
or superseding record.

## Submission

A correction request must identify the target record, explain the alleged error, and provide a
public citation when available. Reporters should not submit personal, confidential, or
access-controlled information.

## Review

1. Acknowledge and assign a durable correction identifier.
2. Preserve the original record and evidence.
3. Assess source authority, directness, entity match, timing, and redistribution rights.
4. Mark materially disputed public claims `contested` while review is active when warranted.
5. Record one disposition: `accepted`, `rejected`, or `applied`, with rationale.
6. Link replacement evidence and superseding assertions without mutating snapshots.

Reviewers with a material conflict of interest must recuse. High-impact disputes require a second
reviewer.

## Publication and audit

Applied corrections identify what changed and when. Rejected requests retain a non-sensitive
rationale. Audit records must not expose reporter contact information. Processing targets and
appeal routes will be published before accepting production correction submissions.
